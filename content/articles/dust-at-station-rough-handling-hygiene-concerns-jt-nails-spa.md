JT Nails & Spa recently received a review from a customer who visited for a plain acrylic set. She wrote the day after her appointment, and her account describes concerns about the condition of the work area, the attention she received, and how her hands were handled. She also shared that she noticed small bumps and itching around her cuticles afterward and was worried about the cause. Her account is a useful reminder that cleanliness and a calm, focused technique are at the heart of every visit.

## A Station Clients Can See Is Clean

According to the customer, the area and the tools the technician was about to use were covered in dust from previous services. She asked whether the dust and the desk condition could cause any harm, and she was told no. She said she felt uneasy, did not want to seem rude, and stayed for the service.

Acrylic work creates fine dust, and clients notice it right away because it sits where their hands rest. A few habits help keep the station fresh. Wipe down the table, the hand rest, and the lamp between every client, and clear dust from the tools before a new service begins. Use a dust collector or a small brush as the work goes on, and replace the towel or disposable cover for each guest. When a client asks about cleanliness, a warm and open answer works best, such as "Thank you for asking, let me wipe everything down fresh for you." Inviting questions in this way helps clients feel comfortable speaking up, rather than staying silent when something worries them.

## Focus and Calm During the Service

The customer also said the technician was watching sports on his phone throughout the service. Clients are very aware of where a technician's attention is, and a visible distraction can make them feel less cared for. A simple team standard, such as keeping phones away from the service area during appointments, helps the focus stay on the client and on the details of the work, from product application to cleanliness.

## Gentle Handling and Respectful Communication

She further described the technician as rough, saying he tapped her hand, pulled her arm, and raised his voice at her twice. She felt the experience was disrespectful.

Hands and wrists are sensitive, and clients usually respond best to slow, steady movements and a friendly tone. Technicians can guide a client's hand with light pressure, ask whether the position is comfortable, and use short, kind phrases such as "Could you hold still for just a moment, please?" If a technician feels frustrated, a short pause or a handoff to a colleague can keep a tense moment from growing. A manager or lead can also check on service areas during busy times to make sure every client feels at ease.

## Responding to Skin Concerns After a Visit

The customer shared that she was waiting to see whether the irritation around her cuticles would get worse. Skin reactions can have many possible causes, so it is not helpful for a salon to guess, but it is important to take every concern seriously. A simple follow-up message the day after a service, an invitation to call with questions, and a suggestion that the client speak with a qualified medical professional if she remains worried all show care. Keeping a record of the products and tools used on each client also helps the team respond clearly if a question comes up.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a reminder that clients judge a salon by what they can see and feel at their own station. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, keep the work area visibly clean, with dust cleared and surfaces wiped between every guest, and welcome questions about cleanliness. Second, keep the technician's full attention on the client, with phones put away during service, and use a gentle, respectful approach to handling. Third, follow up when a client reports any skin concern, listen carefully, and encourage her to seek professional advice if she is worried.

When clients see a clean station and feel calm, careful attention, they have every reason to trust the salon with their hands.
